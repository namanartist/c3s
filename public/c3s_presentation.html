The Core Concept: Why C3S?

On a university campus with thousands of students, accidents, sudden medical emergencies, and escalating altercations demand swift action. Today, an emergency is reported via telephone calls to security guards or wardens. Callers often struggle to explain their exact multi-floor location ("I am somewhere on the 2nd floor of AI block near the corner lab"). Guards spend crucial minutes figuring out which staircase to take.

C3S solves this in 1 tap: Pressing and holding the SOS button for 2 seconds transmits the student's exact floor node, camera snapshot, and initiates a turn-by-turn responder navigation route on guards' screens within 1.2 seconds.

The Problem We Saw on Campus

Ground Realities & The Latency Penalty
FIELD STUDY

1. Phone-Call Delay (8–9 Min)

When a fight breaks out or a student faints in a lab, witnesses panic. Locating security numbers, calling the gate, explaining the scene, and relaying the message to an on-duty guard consumes an average of 537 seconds (8.95 minutes).

By the time responders arrive, injuries worsen or crowds disperse into chaos.

2. Disconnected Security Silos

CCTV cameras record to local DVR hard drives without real-time threat intelligence. Gate registers rely on paper logbooks that cannot be searched during emergencies. Responders communicate over noisy walkie-talkies with zero spatial coordinates.

There is no centralized single-pane-of-glass operating picture.

3. Indoor GPS Blind Spot

Commercial navigation tools like Google Maps and Apple Maps stop at building entrance gates. They have zero visibility into indoor corridors, staircase connections, elevator locations, or classroom numbers across multiple floors.

Responders waste 3–4 minutes simply navigating unfamiliar stairwells.

Three-Tier System Architecture

Presentation • Compute • Persistence + Offline Fallback
ARCHITECTURE

Tier 1: Presentation (Edge)

  • React 19 & TypeScript 6: Strictly typed UI with zero runtime crashes.
  • Vite 8 & Tailwind CSS: High-performance instant bundling and responsive layouts.
  • Zustand State Stores: Granular state slices (safetyStore, mapStore) minimizing re-renders.
  • SVG Vector Map Engine: Real-time path projection and layer switching.

Tier 2: Compute & Dispatch API

  • Node.js Serverless APIs: Modular endpoints in api/*.ts and local dev server.
  • Incident State Machine: Atomic state transitions for incident lifecycles.
  • HMAC-SHA256 Token Engine: Generates tamper-proof QR codes with cryptographic nonces.
  • RBAC Session Middleware: Validates roles (Student, Guard, Gatekeeper, Admin).

Tier 3: Persistence & Storage

  • MongoDB Database: High-throughput collections for nodes, edges, logs, and passes.
  • Optimized Indexes: Spatial index on coordinates and compound index on timestamps.
  • Bundled Graph Fallback: If database connection drops, app loads embedded campus graph instantly.
  • Browser IndexedDB Sync: Stores pending SOS dispatches offline until reconnection.
⚡
Fault-Tolerant Resilience: If the central backend API server crashes or goes offline, student navigation, offline gate ticket reading, and local CCTV frame diffing continue executing in the browser without interruption.

The Mathematics of C3S (Part 1)

Empirical Equations • Response Time, Density & Queuing
MATH MODELS

1. Response Time Model

$$T = t_{\text{detect}} + t_{\text{alert}} + \frac{s}{v_{\text{responder}}}$$
$t_{\text{detect}}$: Time to identify threat (< 2s)
$t_{\text{alert}}$: Latency to dispatch guards (~1.2s)
$s$: Distance along shortest graph path (m)
$v$: Responder sprint velocity (~4 m/s)

By compressing $t_{\text{alert}}$ from 300s (phone call) to 1.2s, total response time drops from 537s to 98s.

2. Crowd Density & Kinetic Jerk

$$\rho = \frac{N}{A} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{J} = \frac{d^2 \mathbf{v}}{dt^2}$$
$\rho$: Crowd density ($\text{people}/\text{m}^2$)
$N$: Detected heads/torso centroids
$A$: Calibrated corridor polygon area
$\mathbf{J}$: 2nd derivative of velocity vector

When kinetic jerk $|\mathbf{J}| > \theta_{\text{threshold}}$, optical flow vectors spike, indicating sudden fighting or fleeing motions.

3. Poisson Incident Arrival

$$P(k) = \frac{(\lambda t)^k e^{-\lambda t}}{k!}$$
$\lambda$: Campus incident arrival rate
$k$: Simultaneous active emergencies
$\mu$: Guard resolution rate ($\mu > \lambda$)

Under M/M/c queuing theory, our multi-guard dispatch queue guarantees finite queue length and zero dropped emergency alerts.

The Mathematics of C3S (Part 2)

Non-Blocking Multi-Floor Dijkstra Graph Routing
GRAPH THEORY

Multi-Floor Shortest Path Algorithm

$$d(v) = \min_{u \in \text{adj}(v)} \{ d(u) + w(u, v) \}$$
$V$: 641 digitized campus nodes
$E$: 1,280 bidirectional weighted edges
$w(u, v)$: Euclidean distance + vertical stair penalty
  • Cross-Floor Edge Weighting: Vertical transitions via stairs add a physical impedance penalty factor ($\Delta z \times 1.5$) to encourage ground-level paths when viable.
  • Step-Free Accessible Routing: Toggling the 'Wheelchair Accessible' switch sets the weight of stair edges to $\infty$, forcing the solver through elevator paths.

Asynchronous Yielding (UI Responsiveness)

In conventional JavaScript, solving large graphs on the main thread triggers noticeable frame drops (> 50ms jank).

if (iterations % 50 === 0) {
  await yieldToMainThread(); // requestAnimationFrame
}

By yielding control every 50 relaxation cycles, the browser maintains a fluid 60 FPS animation loop while computing paths in under 12ms.

Architectural Evolution: Migrating from YOLO11 to OpenCV

Why We Replaced Deep Learning with Edge Computer Vision
KEY INNOVATION

✖ The YOLO11 Bottleneck (Cloud DL)

  • High Inferencing Latency: Video frames had to be compressed, encoded, transmitted over Wi-Fi, and evaluated on a server GPU, introducing 450ms–1,200ms latency.
  • Prohibitive Infrastructure Cost: Hosting a dedicated NVIDIA GPU cluster for 12 simultaneous 1080p camera streams cost upwards of $250/month—infeasible for colleges.
  • Bandwidth Saturation: Continuous video upload choked the MITS academic Wi-Fi network during peak hours.
  • Student Privacy Risk: Live camera footage leaving the campus boundary violates data protection regulations.

✔ The OpenCV Solution (Edge Vision)

  • Sub-2ms Processing: Lightweight frame differencing, background subtraction, and optical flow vectors execute directly in the browser's HTML5 Canvas / WebAssembly.
  • Zero Cloud GPU Cost: Runs on commodity Intel i3/i5 office laptops and guard tablets with less than 8% CPU utilization.
  • Zero Video Bandwidth: Raw video remains inside client memory; only small event JSON payloads (< 1 KB) are transmitted when anomalies occur.
  • 100% Student Privacy: No images or video ever leave the local network boundary without explicit SOS trigger authorization.

Live UI Walkthrough: OpenCV Edge Vision in Action

Baseline Motion vs. Violent Agitation • Real-time Kinetic Bounding Boxes
CV ENGINE
Figure 3: Live camera frame analysis. Left: normal walking traffic (low motion score). Right: high-acceleration fight activity flagged with dynamic red bounding boxes and instant audio-visual alert triggers.

1. Optical Flow Differencing

Consecutive frames are converted to grayscale and differenced ($\Delta I = |I_t - I_{t-1}|$) to detect localized pixel displacement.

2. Kinetic Jerk Detection

Computes acceleration derivatives ($\mathbf{J} = d^2\mathbf{v}/dt^2$). Rapid flailing or grappling creates distinctive high-jerk vector clusters.

3. Instant Threat Escalation

When the motion score exceeds the 75% confidence threshold for 3 consecutive frames, an automated incident ticket is generated for the control room.

Testing, Edge Cases & Honest Limitations

Academic Rigor • Engineering Boundaries
LIMITATIONS

1. Extreme Low-Light & Occlusion

Optical computer vision requires sufficient illumination. In corridors with blown-out bulbs or dense night shadows, optical flow vector quality drops by ~14%.

Mitigation: Fallback to manual SOS button and IR illuminator support.

2. Severe Crowd Occlusion

In extreme bottleneck situations (e.g. auditorium exit stampede), overlapping student silhouettes merge into single contours.

Mitigation: Density estimation $\rho = N/A$ triggers crowd surge alerts rather than individual tracker boxes.

3. Mobile Background Sleep

iOS Safari and Android Chrome aggressively throttle background JavaScript timers after 30 seconds of device screen lock.

Mitigation: Integrated Web Push Notifications and native vibration API wake-locks.

Future Enhancements: Radar Sensing & 3D Positioning

Introducing mmWave Radar • Vertical Z-Axis Discrimination • AR Wayfinding
ROADMAP

Why Radar? (Transcending Camera Boundaries)

While CCTV cameras are restricted by line-of-sight and lighting, millimeter-wave (mmWave) Radar operates at 60 GHz–77 GHz electromagnetic frequencies.

  • Zero Privacy Intrusion: Radars detect point-cloud reflections (Doppler velocity, range, angle) without capturing identifiable facial imagery.
  • Through-Smoke & Through-Wall Sensing: In building fires with heavy smoke or total darkness, radar penetrates obstacles to locate trapped individuals.
  • True 3D Spatial Localization ($x, y, z$): Traditional Wi-Fi trilateration suffers from 5–8 meter vertical drift. Radar point clouds pinpoint the exact elevation altitude ($z$), distinguishing Ground, 1st, 2nd, and 3rd floors unambiguously.

Next-Phase Architecture Integration

  • TI IWR6843 mmWave Radar Nodes: Mounted in corridor ceilings to stream spatial point-cloud clusters over MQTT/WebSockets into C3S.
  • Augmented Reality (AR) Indoor Route Arrows: WebXR overlay on student phone cameras projecting floating 3D directional arrows directly onto real hallway corridors.
  • Autonomous Drone Interceptor: Automatic dispatch of a campus safety drone to provide aerial illumination and live overhead vantage points ahead of ground guards.
